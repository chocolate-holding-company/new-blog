The journey from **Manchester** to **Israel** is not typically one undertaken on two wheels with forty pounds of poorly packed luggage, but on _3_ _September 1991_, that is exactly how the adventure began.

Setting off from **Denton** at the fashionably late hour of 10:30 am—a result of last-minute packing and the realization that a "tonne" of gear actually weighed about 50lb—the first few miles were a brutal education in physics.

The ride to Ironbridge was flat and hot, leaving the cyclist shattered after only _78 miles_, a sobering start given the thousands of miles remaining 1\. The philosophy for the trip was simple: there was no plan, no booked ferries, and no set hostels; the only hard requirement was to be in Israel by the first week of October 2\.

![image caption](/images/pedal-israel/page-two.png)

By the second day, the bike began to show its own personality. A toe clip fell off, and the bike computer failed to connect until it was "cured" by jamming silver paper into the terminals.

After a steep climb out of Ironbridge and a series of busy roads filled with worrying wagons, the cyclist reached **Duntisborne Abbot**. Luck appeared in the form of a sympathetic hostel warden who, recognizing a fellow ex-employee, provided a free night’s stay.

![](/images/pedal-israel/page-three.png)

The English leg concluded with a dash toward the coast. _Passing through the peaceful, tank-guarded Salisbury Plain_—where signs warned "**BEWARE TANKS CROSSING**"—the rider made such good time to **Salisbury** that he decided to aim for the 11:00 pm ferry from **Portsmouth**. This led to a frantic evening ride with legs like _jelly_ and a total lack of lights, save for a head torch. Despite angry drivers beeping and shaking fists at the shadowy cyclist, he reached **Portsmouth** by 9:00 pm, having covered **271 miles** in just three days.

![](/images/pedal-israel/page-four.png)

Boarding the ferry to **France** introduced the first of many _eccentric characters_: a pensioner with a "demob suit," thick spectacles, and a bicycle that looked like a "rag and boneman's" dream. His luggage consisted of _Tesco shopping baskets_ tied to pannier racks, overflowing with suitcases and plastic bags. While other cyclists avoided him, our protagonist listened to the man’s plan to buy a house in **Brittany** and his pride in carrying a _second suit_ for "entertaining the ladies". After a sleepless night huddled under the ferry stairs, the cyclist rolled into **Caen** at 6:00 am, struggling to remember to "**KEEP TO THE RIGHT**".

![](/images/pedal-israel/page-five.png)

**France** offered a mix of "nectar-like" coffee and homicidal truck drivers. On the "A" roads to **Alencon**, massive trucks flew by, seemingly willing to _mow down_ any cyclist in their path. The rider soon realized that straight handlebars were a mistake; his fingers were stiff and aching from the lack of alternative hand positions. He eventually retreated to "B" roads, following a scenic canal route through the **Loire Valley**, though he frequently found himself prohibited from certain roads and lost in the dark without a _decent map_. By the time he reached the mountains at **Lons-le-Saunier**, he faced a 1:2 gradient so steep he had to walk.

![](/images/pedal-israel/page-6.png)

The Alps provided both breathtaking scenery and literal buckets of rain. In _Les Rousses_, 1500 meters above sea level, the cyclist shared a hostel with a _Dutch mountain biker_ who spent the evening lecturing him on the "advantages of paper underpants"—a conversation made even more surreal by the man’s _poor English_. The descent to **Lake Geneva** was a _21km_ "free wheel" that sounds idyllic but was actually a freezing, soaking ordeal as road water sprayed the cyclist’s motionless legs.

![](/images/pedal-israel/page-7.png)

Reaching _Lausanne_, the cyclist stayed in a hostel dominated by a large group of **Algerians**. After a half-hour of frantic hand gestures and broken English, he managed to reclaim his bed from a roommate who had moved into it. Wary of the _political climate_, he decided it was best not to mention his final destination was **Israel**.

![](/images/pedal-israel/page-8.png)

The journey into the **Swiss-Italian** Alps involved a choice between a "spiral" route and a "straight up" route. Opting for the "macho" straight path to **Champex**, the cyclist suffered through a snaking mountain road where he stopped at every bench to _admire the view_ and stop his sweat from drenching the pavement. The scenery was so classic—complete with cowbells and snow-capped peaks—that he half-expected to see _Heidi_ running down the hillside.

![](/images/pedal-israel/page-9.png)

Crossing the **St. Bernard Pass** at _2473 meters_ was a deafening experience due to the echoes in the tunnels, but the summit offered misty, _magnificent_ views into **Italy**.

![](/images/pedal-israel/page-10.png)

**Italy** was a land of speed and regret. On the flat roads toward the coast, the cyclist often maintained _20_ miles per hour.

![](/images/pedal-israel/page-11.png)

However, a disastrous attempt to sleep in a field near **Tromello** resulted in mosquito bites, damp gear, and the _haunting suspicion of rats_. He vowed "never again" to sleep rough in Italy.

![](/images/pedal-israel/page-12.png)

After getting lost in **Bologna**, he eventually reached **Ancona**, exhausted and ready for the _35-hour_ ferry to **Greece**. By this point, _16_ days had passed since **Manchester**, and he had spent **£230**—leading to the grim realization that it would have been cheaper to fly.

![](/images/pedal-israel/page-13.png)

**Greece** offered a brief, _sun-soaked respite_. The cyclist enjoyed _moussaka_—his first cooked meal since **Manchester**—and clear blue waters, though the mosquitoes at **Korinthos** were so aggressive they left his forehead looking like "the Swiss Alps". Navigating the "industrial horror" of **Athens'** outskirts was a nightmare of car horns and a total lack of signage.

![](/images/pedal-israel/page-14.png)

After a final interrogation by **\*Israeli** security* at the **Piraeus** port—who seemingly wanted to know everything down to the "color of his underpants"—*he boarded the final ferry\*.

![](/images/pedal-israel/page-15.png)

The boat stopped at **Cyprus**, where the cyclist was held captive on deck because he hadn't handed his passport to the _purser_. He watched as _hundreds of Russian Jews_ boarded, packing the deck class "like sardines" for the final leg to **Haifa**. On **Thursday** at **7:30 am**, **24** days and **1586** miles after leaving **Denton**, he finally arrived in **Israel**.

![](/images/pedal-israel/page-16.png)

The final push to the kibbutz was marked by the sheer expense of Israeli life and the terrifying habits of local drivers. In Israel, drivers beep their horns constantly, regardless of traffic, which is particularly unnerving when cycling down a mountain in _pitch darkness_ without lights.

The author’s arrival in **Tel-Aviv** revealed a big and very modern city, where he initially sought a place to stay before visiting the _kibbutz office_. He opted for a cheap hostel called the ‘Back pack’, which cost only **15 Shekels** a night but was described as _incredibly scruffy_ and _malodorous_. The premises were overcrowded with _thousands of **Russian** immigrants_ who had flooded the country and were waiting for work, creating an atmosphere the author likened to a _refugee camp_. The hostel was managed by an \*unhappy **American\*** who had lived in **Israel** for _five_ years and appeared "stuck" and perpetually "pissed off," frequently complaining about his life there.

The experience was further defined by the mandatory wait during the **Sabbath** and _subsequent Jewish holidays_, which kept the _kibbutz office_ closed until **Tuesday**. The author admitted he did not particularly like **Tel-Aviv** and found the delay _frustrating_, spending his time sitting on the beach or visiting the park to _pass the hours._ He felt a profound sense of relief upon finally leaving the city for his assignment at **Kibbutz Hagoshrim**, later reflecting that the hostel he found in **Tiberias** was a "palace" compared to the dismal conditions of the ‘Backpack’ in **Tel-Aviv**.

![](/images/pedal-israel/page-17.png)

He eventually reached **Tiberias**, where he met another _English cyclist_ who had lost his bike in **France** because the **\*French** rail system\* sent the bicycle on a different train that never arrived.

![](/images/pedal-israel/page-18.png)

On the _30th day,_ after a final climb out of the **Sea of Galilee**—which sits _211m_ below sea level—the cyclist reached **Kibbutz Hagoshrim**, near the **Lebanese** border.

![](/images/pedal-israel/page-19.png)

The "epic" nature of the _1801-mile journey_ quickly gave way to the mundane reality of _kibbutz life_. Instead of picking fruit in the sunshine, he was assigned to a factory packing _lady shavers_ for eight hours a day.

![](/images/pedal-israel/page-20.png)

Life at **Hagoshrim** was a mix of _boredom,_ _food poisoning_ that "comes out like water," and the occasional "red alert" when terrorists crossed the border in _gliders_. He spent his _Saturdays_ riding up **Mount Hermon** to the _ski resort_ or looking into **Lebanon** from the mountain peaks, finding the scenery _magnificent_ and the local beer "cheap and nasty". After a month of riding across **Europe**, the sudden stop was hard to handle, and within weeks, the cyclist was already planning his next move to a **moshav,** proving that the _spirit of the journey_ was harder to pack away than a 50lb pannier. So off to a _Moshav_ it was then, **but that's another story**….
